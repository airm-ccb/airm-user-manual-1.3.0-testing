# AIRM Change Control Board Terms of Reference 

This document sets out the terms of reference for the AIRM Change Control Board

**`Purpose of the document`** 

The ATM Information Reference Model (`AIRM`) is a key standard within the context of `SWIM` deployment. As such, procedures must be put in place to manage the evolution of the `AIRM` in an effective manner and to ensure that the requirements and views of all stakeholders are taken into account. 

This document sets out the terms of reference for the `AIRM Change Control Board` (`AIRM CCB`). The `AIRM CCB` is the body that has the authority to make decisions on how changes to the `AIRM` should be handled.  

**`Intended readership`** 

The primary intended readership of this document consists of  
- The AIRM community of interest. 


## Purpose and Scope 

The `AIRM Change Control Board` is entrusted with the role of making decisions on how changes to the `AIRM` content should be handled. 

The `AIRM CCB`: 
- sets the main principles that guide the `AIRM` evolution; 
- ensures alignment with the agreed set of related ICAO documents; 
- informs regularly the IMP, ATMRPP, and METP; 
- is responsible for the maintenance of the `AIRM`; 
- prepares the AIRM work plan with `Change Requests` (CR) for next release including prioritisation; 
- prepares a change report for each new `AIRM` release; 
- coordinates with relevant parties (e.g. ICAO); 
- schedules the publication of new release; 
- gives approval to the set of changes included in a release; 
- defines procedures for handling change requests to the `AIRM`; 
- updates the terms of reference as appropriate;   
- publishes procedures on how the `AIRM CCB` runs; 
- manages membership of the `AIRM CCB`;  
- Coordinate the content of the `AIRM Supplements`.


## Composition 

The `AIRM CCB` is composed of SWIM experts and subject matter experts. The `AIRM CCB` is open to relevant members of the ATM Community including, but not limited to members who represent:  
- States; 
- International Organisations; 
- ANSPs; 
- Airports; 
- Airlines; 
- ATM Industry; 
- Communities established around standardised information exchange models; and 
- Military 

Each organisation may be represented by only one member. However, a member may represent an organisation other than their employer
(something which is common for members who, for example, represent international organisations). The `AIRM CCB` ensures the appropriateness 
of the number of members in terms of stakeholder representation whilst ensuring that the `AIRM CCB` proceedings remains workable and practical.  

## CCB Member 

**`AIRM CCB members`** 
- are expected to make an active contribution to the subject content on a voluntary basis; and 
- are responsible for reporting back to their nominating organisation. 

At AIRM CCB meetings, `AIRM CCB members` may be accompanied by advisers and observers. In addition, `AIRM CCB members` may, ad-hoc, bring an expert in support of discussions of a change request. 

**`CCB Chair`**

The `Chair` is elected by the `AIRM CCB members` through majority of votes for an undetermined period. The `Chair`’s responsibility is to seek consensus and buy-in on `AIRM` to the benefit of the international air transport community. It is the `Chair`’s responsibility to ensure that necessary coordination with the relevant parties is effective. 

**`CCB Support Office`** (`CCBSO`)

The `AIRM CCB secretariat support office` is responsible for the day-to-day running of the change management process including: 
- managing and implementing the change requests; 
- organising the change control board meetings; 
- ensuring transparency of `AIRM CCB` proceedings; 
- creating the agenda for meetings; 
- circulating meeting materials; 
- recording the decisions of the meeting; and 
- supporting the `AIRM CCB` related activities of the `AIRM CCB members`. 

It is envisaged that `CCBSO` activities are performed by volunteering member organisations (e.g. EUROCONTROL). 

**`AIRM coordination`**

Coordination with `XM communities`: 
- the purpose is mutual information sharing on XM related CRs and their acknowledgement by the concerned XM community when applicable; 
- the coordination is performed by a liaison with the necessary subject matter expertise and who is active in both the `AIRM CCB` and the concerned XM community. 
- promotes the coordination between `AIRM CCB` and XM communities on practical matters of mutual interest. 

Coordination with the `ICAO IMP` occurs at two levels: 
- IMP observations on work to be done towards the next release, based on the  AIRM work/release plan provided by the AIRM CCB; 
- IMP endorsement on AIRM release, based on the change report provided by the AIRM CCB. 

Note: The `AIRM Change Management Handbook` includes a detailed description of the involved processes outlined in this ToR. 
This includes the AIRM coordination processes that describe the coordination activities in more detail in terms of roles, artefacts and information sharing platforms. 

Note: ICAO internal coordination among the related ICAO panels is not in scope of this ToR. 

## Working Arrangements

**`Meetings`**

The `AIRM CCB` uses: 
- Face-to-face meetings according to a published schedule 
- other working arrangements (e.g. tele-conferences) as agreed by the `AIRM CCB members`. 

The `AIRM CCB` decides by consensus. If no agreement can be found about a particular change request within the `AIRM CCB`, the chair is entrusted to make a decision in the best interests of the AIRM community.  

**`Resources and Cost Covering`** 

The nominating organisation is responsible for covering all costs associated with the member’s contribution to the `AIRM CCB`.  

**`Sunset`**

The `AIRM CCB` will remain active until its members decide that it is no longer required. In order to assess this, the `AIRM CCB` takes into account any evolution of SWIM that may affect the `AIRM`.
