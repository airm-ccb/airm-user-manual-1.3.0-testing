# AIRM Change Management Handbook

The evolution of the AIRM involves the processing of change requests from stakeholder communities. This requires effective and transparent change management to plan,
coordinate with the relevant stakeholder communities, and integrate changes into the AIRM. 

The AIRM Change Management Handbook describes the change management procedures that are used to manage the evolution of the AIRM, 
with due consideration of the requirements and views of the related stakeholder communities. 

Also AIRM change management can be subjected to change, hence this document includes the procedures for those changes.

## Introduction 

### Purpose and objectives

The AIRM Change Management Handbook describes the change management processes that are used to manage the evolution of the AIRM, 
with due consideration of the requirements and views of the related stakeholder communities. 

The set of items subject to these change control process includes: 
- The AIRM itself and supporting documentation; 
- The change management processes themselves, as described in this document. 

For precise details of what objects are subjected to change control, refer to section 5. 

### Intended readership

The intended readership of the AIRM Change Management Handbook consists of stakeholders requesting and proposing changes to the AIRM, 
and experts supporting the AIRM CCB activities involved in deciding and implementing changes to the AIRM.  These stakeholders are defined in detail in section 3, and can be summarised as follows: 
- Stakeholders requesting and proposing changes to the AIRM; 
- AIRM Community Members; 
- AIRM CCB Support Office (CCBSO); 
- AIRM CCB Members;  
- The AIRM Change Request Integration Team; 
- Any other stakeholder interested in joining either the AIRM Community or the AIRM CCB.

## AIRM Change Management Principles 

The change management processes are based on the following principles: 

**`Open-to-community approach`**

An open and inclusive approach is ensured by the AIRM CCB and CCBSO to maximise visibility, transparency and widespread acceptance of the AIRM within the ATM community. 
This requires AIRM CCB and CCBSO coordination with AIRM stakeholder communities including: 
- transparent sharing of information about AIRM evolution; 
- collaborative handling of inputs; 
- management of AIRM  stakeholder community acknowledgements on changes of interest. 

**`Formal decision-making`**

Decisions about changes to and evolution of the AIRM are made by the AIRM CCB, supported by subject matter expertise stemming from the AIRM stakeholder community. 
This allows the CCB to concentrate on decision-making, coordination and planning. 

**`Efficient evolution`** 

Change management processes are streamlined to: 
- Maximise time efficiencies to implement required changes; 
- Maximise cost efficiencies to evolve the AIRM; 
- Minimise coordination overhead for the AIRM community as whole. 

**`Global alignment`**

Change management processes dedicated to coordination with global information exchange models and ICAO ensure the integration of the AIRM in a global context. 

## AIRM Stakeholders 

This section describes the various stakeholders involved in using the AIRM, and evolving the AIRM by requesting and proposing changes to the AIRM. 
The management of the changes is done under the umbrella of the AIRM change management groups described in chapter 4 of this change management handbook. 

### AIRM Community 

Following the principle of an Open-to-Community approach, the “AIRM Community” is the group of AIRM stakeholders that participate in the AIRM processes defined 
in this change management handbook.  AIRM Community members actively contribute to or monitor the evolution of the AIRM.  The contributions made are provided on a royalty-free basis. 

AIRM Community members can submit a Change Request (CR) on behalf of one or several stakeholders. See Chapter 5 for detailed information about how to submit a CR.  

### ICAO and XM communities 

Alignments with the evolution of global XM communities and ICAO documents are drivers for changes to the AIRM. Coordination with the XM communities (AIXM, FIXM and IWXXM) 
ensures the synchronisation of AIRM evolution with the XM communities.  Coordination with ICAO Information Management Panel (IMP) collects planning observations and release 
endorsements from ICAO. See section 5.3 for more information about AIRM coordination.  

## AIRM change management groups 

### AIRM Change Control Board (CCB)  

The AIRM CCB is the body that has the authority to make final decisions on how change requests to the AIRM should be handled, and for overall management of the maintenance and evolution of the AIRM.  
Its roles, responsibilities and composition are described in the AIRM Change Control Board Terms of Reference (ToR). 

### AIRM CCB support office (CCBSO) 

The AIRM CCBSO is responsible for the day-to-day running of the change management process. The CCBSO: 
- Receives the AIRM Change Requests and checks whether they contain enough information to be processed. 
- Organises AIRM Technical Sessions where any member of the AIRM Community can participate to discuss and refine AIRM Change Requests. 
- Submits AIRM Change Requests, which are sufficiently mature for a CCB decision to the CCB. 
- Carries out any other support tasks necessary for efficient operation of the AIRM CCB, as documented in the CCB ToR or in this document. 

The role of the CCBSO is identified in the AIRM Change Control Board Terms of Reference (ToR). In order to carry out its work, the CCBSO will maintain 
the infrastructure and tools necessary for running the change management processes and make these accessible to all relevant stakeholders. 

### AIRM Change Request Integration Team 

The team is composed of people assigned the responsibility for day-to-day maintenance of the AIRM Managed Objects (see Chapter 6), 
including implementation of changes defined in Change Requests approved by the AIRM CCB. 

EUROCONTROL will organise the resources assigned to this team.

In the context of the AIRM Change Management process, a CR is a request to modify a managed object.  CRs must be described with reference to the latest version of the managed object. Typically a CR will express a need requiring change. 

Initially, a CR does not necessarily contain a specific proposal for the exact details of the change that addresses the need for change. It is sufficient for initial registration purposes that the CR describes the need for a specific change of a managed object supported by a rationale.  For example, a CR may initially point out a problem - implying that an AIRM update is required – but without suggestions about what updates might address the problem. 

Before a CR can be considered for approval by the CCB, it must contain a Change Proposal (CP).  A CP is a CR that contains a section that describes one or more specific changes to a Managed Object.  A CP is not a separate type of document, it is a type of CR. 

In some cases, a Requester will have inputs for specific changes from the start and will produce a CP as soon as it is created.  In other cases, the Requester may have identified a shortcoming but feel unable to propose specific changes when initially formulating the CR.  In such cases the CR will evolve to a CP at a later stage, for instance with help from the CCBSO or a Technical Session (see below). 

A CR is initially created by filling in the fields in a Change Request Template, downloaded from the AIRM Community Area.  The AIRM Community Area is an online collaboration platform where CRs are stored and managed; details of how to access and use the platform are provided in Appendix A.

### AIRM coordination processes 

This section describes the AIRM coordination processes related to XM communities of interest and ICAO IMP. Details about these coordination processes are elaborated in Appendix E. 

#### XM coordination 

The AIRM CCB coordinates with other XM communities to ensure necessary alignments. 

The coordination can be performed by the means of a liaison, i.e. an individual who participates in both AIRM and a XM community.  

The liaison will be proposed by the AIRM CCB and should be accepted by both the AIRM CCB and the concerned XM community.  

The liaison with a specific XM community will: 
- Ensure that the XM-related AIRM change proposals are acknowledged by the XM community; 
- Submit the XM-related AIRM change proposals to the AIRM CCB and participate in the discussion on these change proposals on behalf of the XM community1; 
- Communicate and coordinate the outcome with the XM community; 
- Communicate the AIRM work- and release plan and collect observations from the XM community.

#### IMP coordination 

The AIRM is expected to be referenced by ICAO PANS-IM procedures. AIRM change management coordination with ICAO, is facilitated through the the ICAO Information Management Panel (IMP).   In practice, and in accordance with the AIRM CCB ToRs, this implies that the AIRM CCB coordinates with the ICAO IMP.  

In practice, this coordination will be performed as follows: 
- The ICAO Information Management Panel (IMP) will be kept regularly updated including:  awareness of CRs that have been approved by the AIRM CCB and where applicable, with acknowledgement information of the related stakeholder communities (e.g. XM CCB); 
- For this purpose the AIRM CCB/CCBSO will provide an AIRM work/release plan to the ICAO IMP portal; 
- When AIRM changes have been implemented, and before the release of a new AIRM version, a change report will be produced and communicated to the ICAO IMP.  Technically, the purpose of doing this is to inform ICAO, about AIRM evolutions and enable its referencing as deemed necessary by ICAO publications. Note: this does not prevent the AIRM CCB to publish a new release should it deem necessary. 
- For this purpose the AIRM CCB/CCBSO will provide an AIRM work/release plan to the ICAO IMP portal:  
  - IMP observations on the AIRM work/release plan will be collected on the ICAO Portal for subsequent use of the AIRM CCB.
  - The provision of the AIRM work/release plan is decoupled from the ICAO IMP meetings.  
- At IMP WG meetings an AIRM evolution Working Paper (WP) is presented by the AIRM CCB liaison of the IMP to summarize the work and enable the IMP to issue additional observations and endorsements.  

Note: it is assumed that the IMP performs internal ICAO coordination with other Panels (notably the ATM Requirements and Performance Panel (ATMRPP) and the MET Panel, and internally ensures coordination with its Working Group A.  
 
### CR implementation process 

This section describes the CR implementation process and its relationship to a given official AIRM Release.  

When available, a number of CRs are presented and discussed at each CCB meeting. When approved, the AIRM CR Integration Team will integrate the approved CRs into a working version of the AIRM. The working versions will not be released.  

Before a new AIRM release, AIRM CCB can decide on a proposed release (version) that will be shared with ICAO IMP for endorsement. The proposed release will contain the implemented changes since the previous AIRM release,  

When an official AIRM version/release is published, the new release will contain a release note including the changes that have been integrated since the last AIRM release.

### AIRM Supplements 

The AIRM, as a global reference for the ATM vocabulary, is expected to exhibit the concepts and definitions that are relevant to all ATM actors worldwide. This includes concepts and definitions that are applicable at global level and may include concepts and definitions that meet the requirements of specific geographic region (where no requirements were defined at the ICAO level) but that are still relevant to ATM actors such as airlines which commonly operate in several countries and/or regions.  

When included in the AIRM, the concepts and definitions that meet the requirements of specific geographic regions are captured in AIRM Supplements, which form an integral part of the AIRM but allow these concepts to be clearly delineated. 

It is expected that each AIRM Supplement: 
- the content of each AIRM Supplement is managed by a community of interest (e.g. Region) that developed it, this entails: 
  - providing the supplement for inclusion into the AIRM, and;  
  - providing its subsequent potential changes. 
- the AIRM CCB ensures that any AIRM Supplement is in accordance with the AIRM rules applicable to Supplements. 
- if the community of interest has no representation in the AIRM CCB, a representative of the community of interest will be encouraged to join the AIRM CCB to 
  - ensure that all AIRM CCB members remain informed about any AIRM Supplement changes;  
  - coordinate with the AIRM CCB on the evolution of the Supplement. 

## AIRM Technical Sessions 

### Purpose 

The purpose of a Technical Session is to improve the quality of one or more CRs, so that each CR: 
- is sufficiently mature and complete to be submitted to the CCB for an approval/rejection decision; 
- takes into account inputs and observation from stakeholders with specialist technical knowledge on the subject of the CR. 

### Participation 

Participants in a Technical Session are normally staff from the CCBSO or members of the AIRM Community. 

Exception:  For CRs dealing with Management Components (see section 5.3), CCB members will also take part, and AIRM Community Members would not normally be involved. 

However, participation is not strictly limited to AIRM Community Members (i.e. registered in the AIRM Community Area).  AIRM Community Members are encouraged to forward announcements of Technical Sessions to relevant people in their organizations who might be able to provide specialist expertise on topics relevant to the CRs to be discussed. 

AIRM Community members are entitled to take part in any Technical Session, but the CCBSO will try to ensure that at least some of the attendees at a given session have a high level of expertise and interest in the specific CRs to be discussed.    

### Organisation 

It is the CCBSO that decides when a Technical Session is needed and which CRs will be discussed.  The CCBSO will normally group a number of CRs (preferably related) to be discussed during the same Technical Session. 

The CCBSO will announce the Technical Session in the AIRM Calendar and using such other means as appropriate (e.g. email).  The announcement will include:  
- CR(s) to be discussed (ID and Title); 
- format of the Technical Session (see below); 
- timeframe for the meeting (note that sessions using the discussion board format may span periods of many days or even weeks); 
- all practical details needed to take part in the session. 

The CCBSO will supervise discussions at Technical Sessions, acting as a facilitator and providing the participants with advice on AIRM procedures and best practices.  

### Formats 

The CCBSO can suggest different formats for Technical Sessions. The formats shown in the sub-sections below have traditionally been used in the past and are the most likely to be used in the future.  However, the CCBSO is entitled to propose novel formats other than those below if they are perceived to offer substantial benefits. 

#### Discussion Board Format 

This format is favoured in order to maximize visibility and minimize costs.  It provides an asynchronous communication format that allows all views to be recorded and reviewed by all participants and allows high flexibility in attendance as sessions span multiple days and people can join and leave as required.  Experience has shown that this format is suitable for many (but not all) CRs. 

The CCBSO will create a discussion board in the AIRM Community Area General Discussions Board. 

Exception:  For CRs dealing with Management Components (see section 5.3), the thread will be created in the AIRM Community Area CCB Discussions Board. 

The discussion board will include exactly one discussion thread for each CR. 

The CCBSO will include a link to the discussion board in the AIRM Calendar announcement of the Technical Session.   

One week is the default duration for a discussion board Technical Session. Exceptionally, the CCBSO may propose a shorter or longer duration.   

#### Teleconference Format 

For some types of CRs, a more dynamic, synchronous and interactive communication style is needed than can be achieved with the Discussion Board format.  In such cases, Teleconferencing will be used to maximize visibility and minimize costs. 

If the CCBSO decides that this format is suitable for the CR(s) to be discussed, the CCBSO will ensure that the connection details are provided in the announcement of the session.  The technology used to support teleconferencing will be determined by the CCBSO, based on what is most practical for the participants. The technology must as a minimum support audio communication and screen-sharing. (Video communication is not required). 

#### Face-to-face Meeting Format 

In the case of particularly complex CRs, the CCBSO may decide that the most efficient way to make progress on CRs is to have a face-to-face meeting. This should only be done if it is clear that the other formats will not be sufficient (or if they have already been tried and failed). 

If the CCBSO decides that this format is suitable for the CR(s) to be discussed, the CCBSO will first make an initial assessment of the people whose attendance will be required, then start a process to determine possible dates that meet the availability constraints of these people.  In selecting a date and location the CCBSO will attempt, if feasible, to co-locate with other events that participants may be attending. 

The announcement for the session will include details of place and date, and any practical information needed by participants in order to attend. 

### Conclusion of Technical Sessions 

The CCBSO will participate actively in all Technical Sessions, regardless of the format. 

On completion of a Technical Session, the CCBSO will consider each CR in turn and: 
- Evaluate the discussions and suggestions made during the session related to the CR. 
- Update the CR to reflect the outcomes of the CR as accurately as possible: 
  - In cases where a clear way forward was determined in the session:  carry out detailed updates to the CR, making it ready for consideration by the CCB; 
  - In cases where there have been disagreements in the session: 
     - Add notes to the CR (for the benefit of the Requestor and the CCB) to indicate any difficulties encountered with the CR; 
     - Try to form a consensus/compromise view and use this to carry out a detailed update of the CR; 
     - EITHER pass to the CCB OR postpone for further discussion. 

## AIRM CCB decisions and resolutions 

### CCB decision-making  

In line with the AIRM CCB ToR [1], the AIRM CCB decides by consensus. If no agreement can be found about a particular change request within the AIRM CCB, the chair is entrusted to make a decision in the best interests of the ATM community.  

### CCB conflict resolution 

Decisions of the AIRM CCB are final and can only be re-assessed in the event of exceptional circumstances, for instance due to a persisting disagreement on an AIRM CCB decision, the CCB Chairperson can be contacted privately.  The Chairperson will then undertake to communicate any concerns in a clear, independent and constructive way to the next meeting of the CCB. The aim is then to resolve the concern in a way that is acceptable to all parties.  

### CCB working arrangements 

#### CCB meetings frequency 

The CCBSO determines the frequency of CCB meetings, but there must be at least one per year. 

The normal expected frequency is one meeting every 3 months. 

Meeting dates will normally be planned one meeting in advance i.e. part of the agenda of each meeting will be to determine the date of the next meeting.  However, CCB members can request meetings at other times, and in such cases the CCBSO will coordinate with members to identify a suitable date. 

#### CCB meetings attendance  

CCB meetings are open to all members of the CCB. However, it is not mandatory that all members should attend all meetings. The CCBSO will always make it clear, in the invitation to attend a CCB meeting, which topics will be discussed (including which CRs are to be considered) at that meeting. An individual CCB member can use that information to decide whether or not to attend. Where the attendance of a particular member is considered by the CCBSO to be of particular importance for a specific meeting, the CCBSO will make the member aware of this in the invitation. 

CCB members who are unable to attend a meeting are free to send their input/opinions to the meeting beforehand, and the CCB Chairman undertakes to make sure that these are presented to the meeting.  CCB members can also appoint someone else as their “deputy” for a particular meeting, by informing the CCB Chairman beforehand. 

If a CCB meeting is attended by fewer than five CCB members, the decisions that are taken during CCB meeting will be shared with all CCB members for feedback. In the case that a CCB member (who was not able to attend the meeting) disagrees with the decisions, (s)he should provide his feedback within three weeks after the meeting. The CCBSO will coordinate with the member concerned and organise additional session if needed.    

Persons other than the official CCB members can, where necessary, attend meetings of the CCB in order to present technical details so that participants can better understand key issues. Any such attendees are not allowed to participate in decision-making process - they can only provide technical clarifications to assist in the decision-making process. 

#### CCB meeting format 

The teleconference format will be the preferred format for most CCB meetings.  Remarks about this format defined for Technical Sessions (see section 7.4.2) also apply to CCB meetings. 

There will be at least one face-to-face meeting per year, as this format is best suited to more complex and wide-ranging discussions.  Remarks about this format defined for Technical Sessions (see section 7.4.3) also apply to CCB meetings, apart from the part that says the format should only be used if other formats have failed. 

#### AIRM CCB meeting contents 

Each CCB meeting can contain one of each of the following: 

`General Session`

Potential topics to be discussed: 
- CRs that require a decision 
- Release planning and review 
- Directions for CCBSO 
- Coordination with ICAO and other stakeholder communities 
- Other 

`CR Presentation Session`

When a CR Approval Session (see below) is planned for a given CCB meeting, the CCBSO will include a CR Presentation Session on the agenda before the CR Approval Session.  This session will provide a concise summary of the CRs, to assist CCB members in making decisions.  In some cases, a pre-CCB CR presentation may also have been arranged – see section 8.3.5 below. The CR Presentation Session includes the demonstration of CR acknowledgements captured from related stakeholder communities such as the XM CCBs. 

`CR Approval Session` 

The purpose of an approval session is to make a decision about each CR with status “CCB”. It will normally be preceded by a CR Presentation Session in the CCB meeting itself, and there may also have been a pre-CCB CR Presentation Session some weeks before (see section 8.3.5). 

Based on the CCB Presentation Session(s), CCB members will be asked to agree on how to handle each CR (i.e. Approve or Reject). If no consensus is reached, the following options are foreseen: 
- Postpone any decision on the CR. 
- Return CR to CCBSO for further Technical Sessions (requires a CCB statement). 

After the meeting, the CCBSO will document the decisions made in the meeting (primarily by updating the “status” and other fields in the CRs), and communicate the updates to the Requestors. 

The aim is that the CCB will deal with all CRs with status “CCB” in each meeting. If time does not permit this, then the status of the unhandled CRs remains unchanged, and the CCBSO informs the Requestors that their CRs must wait until the next CCB meeting. 

It is important to mention that a CR Approval Session is not a session to discuss improvements to a CR.  If the CCB feel that further technical discussion is needed before being able to make a decision, then the CR should be returned to the CCBSO to arrange a Technical Session. 

`Technical Session for a Management Component`

A CCB meeting can be used to host a Technical Session for a CR on a Management Component. 

#### Pre-CCB CR Presentation Session 

When needed (e.g. for particularly complex CRs,) the CCBSO may decide to arrange a CR presentation session approximately two weeks before the CCB meeting.  This will be done using teleconferencing. For each CR the following information will be presented: 
- ID 
- Title 
- Short summary 
- Involved organisations 
- Coordination acknowledgements from relate stakeholder community (e.g. XM CCB) 
- Date of submission  
- Summary of conclusions reached at Technical Sessions 
- CCBSO Recommendations  

The CCBSO will make the relevant materials available for the CCB members in advance. 

Note that a CR Presentation Session is not a session to discuss improvements to a CR – that is the purpose of Technical Sessions (see section 7).  The CR presentation session is rather aimed at providing CCB members with key information about the CR in order to help them to make a decision on the CR.
